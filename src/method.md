A diagnosis names the mechanism. Commentary tracks the symptom. Mechanism generalizes: understand what produces a class of outcomes, and you can anticipate the next instance before it arrives.

Three claims underlie everything here. Dispute any of them and the conclusions don't follow.

**Coercive exclusion requires a legitimate basis.** The defensible one is prevention of genuine conflict: two people cannot simultaneously occupy the same land, so a rule about priority is necessary. That is the entire justification for exclusive claims enforced by the state. Where that basis is absent (over ideas that replicate without loss, over built environments imposed without consent, over political access restricted to protect incumbents), the exclusion is privilege, not property.

**Power that cannot be escaped optimizes for extraction, not service.** Competition creates accountability because the cost of failure is losing to someone better. Remove the exit through legal monopoly, coercive planning, regulatory capture, or infrastructure control, and accountability disappears. What replaces it is the same in every case.

**The standard explanation for a persistent failure tends to serve whoever benefits from its continuation.** Sustained extraction at scale requires a story that doesn't identify it as extraction. "Market failure" obscures the state grant behind the monopoly. "Corporate greed" obscures the privilege that makes greed effective. "Polarization" obscures the structure producing unresponsive incumbents. The diagnosis begins by questioning the frame.

Six areas where the mechanism is most visible, most consequential, and most systematically attributed to the wrong cause:

**State Monopolies:** the grant directly. How governments create and protect concentrated market power, and what happens to everyone the grantee no longer needs to compete for.

**Intellectual Property:** patent, copyright, and trademark as state-enforced knowledge enclosure. Not property rights. Their opposite.

**Cognitive Capture:** how institutions protect the epistemic conditions for their own legitimacy. Control which framings enter mainstream discourse, and the structural critique never accumulates enough pressure to threaten the incumbent arrangement.

**Urban Planning:** the built environment as deliberate state output. Car dependency, housing scarcity, geographic segregation by use: these were chosen by planners and legislators, not by the people who live inside them.

**Technology and Sovereignty:** who owns the infrastructure, what they do with the monopoly once they have it, and why open-source and decentralization are political arguments, not technical preferences.

**Electoral Systems:** how incumbents insulate themselves from the competitive pressure elections are supposed to apply. Gerrymandering, ballot access barriers, primary design.

These overlap. IP law is a monopoly grant applied to knowledge. Car-dependent infrastructure is subsidized monopoly applied to space. Cognitive capture is how the arrangement defends itself from diagnosis.
